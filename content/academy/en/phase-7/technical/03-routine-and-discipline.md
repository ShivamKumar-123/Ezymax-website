Most traders know what they should do. The difficulty is doing it every day, including the days when they are tired, bored or still annoyed about yesterday's loss. Relying on willpower for every decision is exhausting and unreliable. Professionals rely on routine instead: the same sequence of steps before, during and after every session, so that good behaviour becomes the default rather than an effort.

## Why routine beats willpower

Every decision uses mental energy. A trader who must decide each morning which markets to watch, how much to risk and when to stop will make worse choices by the afternoon. A routine removes those decisions. It also creates checkpoints where problems are caught early: an overlooked central-bank decision, an account still carrying yesterday's position, or a trader who slept four hours and should not be trading at all.

## The pre-session checklist

Keep it short enough to finish in 15 to 30 minutes. A typical checklist:

1. **Readiness.** Rested, not distracted, no emotional carry-over from the last session. If not, trade smaller or not at all.
2. **Account state.** Open positions, pending orders, margin level and balance in Kalks Trader. Confirm nothing unexpected is open.
3. **Calendar.** High-impact releases today on the currencies and markets you trade, from the economic calendar in the Client Area. Note their server times.
4. **Context.** Higher-timeframe trend and key levels on each symbol in your plan, marked on the chart.
5. **Scenarios.** For each symbol, what you would need to see to act, and what would keep you out.
6. **Limits.** Today's maximum loss and maximum number of trades, written down.

> **Tip:** Save your chart layouts in Kalks Trader with the levels already drawn, so the checklist starts from a prepared screen rather than a blank one.

## Trading windows and server time

Setups tend to work best in specific liquidity conditions. A breakout strategy on GBPUSD needs the London session; an index strategy may depend on the US cash open. Define your window in server time so your routine matches the platform clock.

| Event (during US daylight saving time) | Local time | Kalks server time |
|---|---|---|
| London session opens | 08:00 London | 10:00 |
| Major US data releases | 08:30 New York | 15:30 |
| US cash equity open | 09:30 New York | 16:30 |
| US cash equity close | 16:00 New York | 23:00 |
| New York close, daily candle roll | 17:00 New York | 00:00 |

Because the server shifts with US daylight saving, the New York times above map to the same server times all year, while London times can move by an hour for a few weeks each spring and autumn when the two regions change clocks on different dates.

> **Example:** A trader whose plan covers GBPUSD from 10:00 to 13:00 server time finds from the journal that 70% of losing trades were taken after 13:00, during the quiet period before New York. Ending the window at 13:00 without exceptions removes most of those trades.

## Discipline during the session

Once the window opens, the job is execution, not analysis from scratch. Useful rules:

- Place stop loss and take profit with every order, so risk is fixed on the server.
- Log each trade in the journal as it is opened, including the reason, not at the end of the day from memory.
- Stop after the daily loss limit or maximum number of trades, even if a perfect setup appears five minutes later.
- Step away from the screen for a few minutes after any loss larger than 1R before taking another trade.
- Avoid checking unrelated charts "just in case"; that is how unplanned trades begin.

> **Risk warning:** Leveraged CFD trading can produce fast losses. Routines reduce avoidable mistakes but do not remove market risk, and a disciplined trader can still have losing days and weeks.

## The post-session routine

Close the day deliberately, in about 15 minutes:

1. Confirm open positions and pending orders are intended, with stops in place.
2. Complete journal entries, adding screenshots and a grade for rule adherence.
3. Note one thing done well and one to improve.
4. Check tomorrow's calendar and mark any releases that affect overnight positions.
5. Close the platform. Watching positions all evening rarely improves them.

## Common mistakes

- Treating the routine as optional on busy days, which are exactly the days it matters most.
- A checklist so long it is skipped. Ten items is plenty.
- Trading outside the window because nothing happened inside it. A day with no trades that followed the plan is a successful day.
- Forgetting that server time, not your local clock, sets daily candles, swap time and the trading week.
