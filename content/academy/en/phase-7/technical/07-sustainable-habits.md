Many traders can have a good month. Far fewer are still trading, with their capital and their health intact, five years later. Sustainability is not a soft topic added at the end of a course. An edge only pays off across hundreds of trades, so anything that forces you to stop, whether a blown account, burnout or a financial emergency, destroys the edge along with it. This chapter pulls together the psychology track into habits that last.

## Realistic expectations

Unrealistic expectations are the root of many destructive behaviours. A trader who expects to double the account in a few months will size positions too large and will view every normal losing week as failure. Professional money managers who achieve steady, modest annual returns with controlled drawdowns are considered successful.

There is no standard return you should expect, and no strategy is guaranteed to be profitable. What you can decide is the level of risk you are willing to take, and that should come from the drawdown you can tolerate, not from the income you would like.

> **Risk warning:** CFDs are leveraged products and many retail traders lose money. Treat trading capital as money you can afford to lose, and never borrow to trade or rely on trading income for essential expenses.

## Process goals over outcome goals

Outcome goals such as "make 1,000 USD this month" depend on the market and tempt you to force trades when it offers nothing. Process goals depend only on you. A weekly scorecard keeps attention on them.

| Process goal | This week |
|---|---|
| Pre-session checklist completed before every session | 5 of 5 |
| Every order placed with stop loss and take profit | 11 of 11 |
| No trades outside the plan's setups and window | 10 of 11 |
| Journal complete, with grade, by end of day | 4 of 5 |

Scores like these tell you whether you are building the habits that produce results over time. A profitable week with poor process scores is a warning; a losing week with perfect process scores is often just variance.

## Scaling gradually and by rule

Sizing up too fast is one of the most common ways that a sound strategy fails. A sensible path has stages, each with written criteria to move forward:

1. **Demo.** Trade the plan on a free demo account in Kalks Trader until you have a meaningful sample, for example 50 to 100 trades, with good rule adherence.
2. **Small live.** Trade the same plan live at a fraction of normal risk, for example 0.25% per trade, or on a cent account where balances are shown in USC. The aim is to experience real emotions and real execution, not to make money.
3. **Normal live.** Move to full planned risk only after a further sample in which statistics and discipline hold up.

Within live trading, fixed-fractional sizing already scales with the account:

```text
Risk 1% of current balance
Balance 10,000 USD -> risk 100 USD per trade
Balance 12,000 USD -> risk 120 USD per trade
Balance  9,000 USD -> risk  90 USD per trade

A 25-pip EURUSD stop at 12,000 USD:
120 / (25 x 10 USD) = 0.48 lot
```

The growth is gradual and automatic. What breaks traders is not this steady scaling but a sudden jump, such as moving from 1% to 3% risk after a good month.

## Money habits: separation and withdrawals

Keep trading capital separate from money you need for living costs. When rent depends on this month's result, every trade carries pressure that distorts judgement. Many traders also set a withdrawal policy, such as withdrawing a fixed share of profits at the end of each quarter through the Client Area. This turns account growth into something tangible and reduces the temptation to keep increasing size.

## Health, attention and time

Decision quality depends on the state of the person making the decisions. Poor sleep, long unbroken screen sessions and high stress all reduce attention and self-control, and they tend to show up in the journal as C-grade trades.

- Trade within a defined window rather than all day. A two- or three-hour session with full attention usually beats eight hours of partial attention.
- Take short breaks away from the screen, especially after a loss or a large win.
- Avoid trading when ill, exhausted or emotionally upset. Not trading is a valid decision and costs nothing.
- Keep regular exercise and time away from markets. Stop loss, take profit and pending orders in Kalks Trader are held on the server, so there is no need to watch every tick.
- Take occasional full breaks from trading, such as a week off each quarter, to reset.

## In practice

Sustainable trading looks unremarkable from the outside: the same routine each day, modest risk, a journal kept up to date, a monthly review, gradual changes and regular time away. It rarely feels exciting. That is a sign it is working. The next phase, on professional trading, builds on these habits to cover portfolios of strategies, prop-firm rules and managing capital for others.

## Common mistakes

- Setting monthly income targets and forcing trades to meet them.
- Increasing risk sharply after a winning streak.
- Treating demo results as proof and going live at full size.
- Ignoring sleep and stress, then blaming the strategy for the decisions they caused.
