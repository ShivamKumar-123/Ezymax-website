Crude oil is the world's most important commodity. It fuels transport, feeds plastics and chemicals, and appears directly in every consumer price index. That gives oil a double role in intermarket analysis: its price reflects the health of global demand, and its moves feed back into inflation, interest rates and currencies. On Ezymex you can trade it through USOIL and UKOIL.

## The two benchmarks

- **WTI (West Texas Intermediate)**, shown as USOIL, is a light, low-sulphur US crude priced for delivery at Cushing, Oklahoma.
- **Brent**, shown as UKOIL, is based on North Sea crude and is the reference for most of the world's seaborne oil.

They usually move in the same direction. The **Brent-WTI spread**, typically a few dollars with Brent above WTI, widens or narrows with US production, pipeline and export capacity, and events in the Middle East or Europe. A supply shock in a shipping lane tends to affect Brent first; a problem at US storage hubs affects WTI first.

> **Note:** Oil CFDs are commonly based on futures contracts, which expire monthly. Depending on how the symbol is built, your position may be subject to a roll adjustment or the symbol may track a specific contract month. ## What drives the price

Oil is priced where supply meets demand, and both sides can shift quickly.

| Supply side | Demand side |
|---|---|
| OPEC+ production quotas and meetings | Global GDP growth and manufacturing activity |
| US shale output and rig counts | Chinese imports and refinery runs |
| Geopolitical disruption, sanctions, shipping attacks | Seasonal driving demand in the northern summer |
| Strategic reserve releases or refills | Recession fears and demand destruction at high prices |

OPEC+ decisions are scheduled events. A surprise production cut can lift prices several dollars in a session; a decision to raise output does the opposite. Between meetings, comments from key energy ministers can move the market.

## Weekly inventory reports

Every week the US publishes data on crude and fuel stocks. The American Petroleum Institute (API) releases private estimates on Tuesday afternoon New York time, and the official Energy Information Administration (EIA) report follows on Wednesday at 10:30 New York time. The market compares the actual change with expectations, just as with any economic release.

> **Example:** Analysts expect a 1.0 million barrel build in US crude stocks. The EIA reports a 5.0 million barrel draw. USOIL rises from 78.00 to 80.40 within fifteen minutes. On an example contract of 1,000 barrels per lot (check the specification for your account), a 0.10 lot position controls 100 barrels, so the $2.40 move is worth 100 x $2.40 = $240.

## Oil in the intermarket chain

Oil connects to the other markets covered in this section:

1. **Inflation and yields.** Energy moves headline CPI directly and indirectly through transport and production costs. A sustained oil rally lifts inflation expectations, raises breakeven rates and can push yields up, which is negative for bonds.
2. **Central banks.** Policymakers usually look through short-lived energy spikes, but a persistent rise can delay rate cuts, supporting the currency of the affected economy.
3. **Currencies of exporters.** CAD and NOK tend to benefit from higher oil, as covered in the chapter on commodity currencies.
4. **Currencies of importers.** Japan imports almost all of its energy and India imports most of its crude. Higher oil worsens their trade balances, which tends to weigh on JPY and INR. USDINR often drifts higher during oil rallies, although the Reserve Bank of India frequently intervenes to smooth the move.
5. **Equities.** Energy producers benefit, while airlines, transport and consumer sectors are hurt. For broad indices, a demand-driven rally (strong growth) is usually benign; a supply-driven spike acts like a tax on consumers and can weigh on stocks.

## Supply shock or demand story?

The most important question when oil moves is **why**. A rally driven by strong global demand usually comes with rising equities and firm commodity currencies. A rally driven by a supply disruption can come with falling equities, rising inflation expectations and safe-haven buying of gold. The same oil chart can mean opposite things for the rest of your watchlist.

## Common mistakes

- **Trading through inventory releases without a plan.** Spreads widen and price can jump several dollars; a tight stop may be hit by the initial spike.
- **Ignoring contract details.** Contract size, trading hours and roll treatment differ between symbols and accounts.
- **Assuming USOIL and UKOIL are different trades.** They are highly correlated; holding both in the same direction roughly doubles your oil exposure.
- **Forgetting the triple swap.** On Ezymex energies carry triple swap on Friday night, which affects the cost of holding positions over the weekend.

> **Risk warning:** Oil can gap sharply on OPEC+ announcements and weekend geopolitical events. CFDs are leveraged, and a gap through your stop can produce a loss larger than planned.
