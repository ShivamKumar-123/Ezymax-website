Most FX analysis looks at weeks or months. Yet the largest moves in currency markets are long cycles that last many years. EURUSD traded near 0.83 in 2000, near 1.60 in 2008 and below parity in 2022. A trader who understood which phase of the dollar cycle they were in could lean with the larger tide instead of against it.

## The dollar cycle in history

Since exchange rates began floating in the early 1970s, the broad US dollar has moved in long waves:

| Period | Dollar trend | Main drivers |
|---|---|---|
| 1980 to 1985 | Strong rise | Very high US real rates under tight Fed policy |
| 1985 to 1992 | Decline | Coordinated intervention (Plaza Accord), falling US rates |
| 1995 to 2001 | Rise | US productivity boom, equity inflows |
| 2002 to 2011 | Decline | Global growth led by emerging markets, low US rates, commodity boom |
| 2011 to 2022 | Rise | US growth and tech outperformance, higher US yields, safe-haven demand |

Each upswing and downswing lasted roughly six to ten years, with sharp counter-trend moves inside them. These dates are approximate; different dollar indices mark the turns slightly differently.

## What drives a long cycle

Four forces tend to explain most of the long waves:

- **Relative growth.** When the US grows faster than the rest of the world, capital flows into US assets.
- **Real interest rate differentials.** Higher inflation-adjusted yields attract fixed income and carry flows. The real differential matters more than the nominal one, because a high nominal rate with higher inflation offers no real return.
- **Capital flows and valuations.** Large equity inflows into a booming sector, such as US technology, support the currency. When those assets become expensive relative to the rest of the world, the flow can reverse.
- **Risk appetite and the dollar's reserve role.** In a crisis, global borrowers who owe dollars need to buy them, which strengthens the currency even if the crisis starts in the US.

## The dollar smile

The dollar smile is a simple way to combine these forces. Picture a smile-shaped curve.

- **Left side:** global crisis and risk aversion. The dollar strengthens because of safe-haven demand and dollar funding needs.
- **Bottom:** synchronised global growth where the US is not outperforming. Capital leaves for higher returns abroad and the dollar weakens.
- **Right side:** strong US outperformance with rising US yields. The dollar strengthens on growth and rate advantages.

This explains why the dollar rose in both 2008 (crisis) and 2022 (US outperformance with an aggressive Fed), and fell in 2003 to 2007 and in parts of 2017 (synchronised global growth). For your own analysis, ask which part of the smile the world is closest to today, and what would move it along the curve.

## Valuation: purchasing power parity

Purchasing power parity (PPP) states that over the long run exchange rates should move towards the level that equalises the price of the same basket of goods in both countries. International organisations publish PPP estimates.

```text
PPP estimate of EURUSD          1.2500
Spot EURUSD                     1.0850
Spot relative to PPP            1.0850 / 1.2500 = 0.868
Euro valuation vs PPP           0.868 - 1 = -13.2%  (euro undervalued)
```

A 13% gap looks like a strong signal, but PPP deviations of 20% or more have lasted for years. The euro stayed well below PPP estimates for most of the 2015 to 2024 period. PPP is best used to judge how stretched a long trend is. Late in a dollar bull market, large PPP gaps and expensive US asset valuations are warning signs that the cycle may be maturing, even if they cannot tell you the month it turns.

## Applying the cycle to shorter-term trading

> **Example:** A trader's macro review concludes that the dollar is in the later stage of a long bull cycle: the US real rate advantage is narrowing as the Fed cuts, the euro is 13% below PPP, and global growth is broadening. That does not justify an immediate large EURUSD long. Instead the trader adopts a mild bullish EURUSD bias: long setups on the daily chart are taken at full size, short setups at half size with tighter targets, and the bias is reviewed monthly. If US data re-accelerate and the real yield gap widens again, the bias is dropped.

Other pairs have their own cycles. USDJPY is heavily driven by the US-Japan rate gap and Japanese investor flows. Commodity currencies such as AUDUSD and USDCAD follow the commodity cycle. Gold, priced in dollars, has often moved inversely to the long dollar cycle.

## Common mistakes

- **Calling the top too early.** Long cycles overshoot fair value, often by a wide margin.
- **Using nominal rather than real rates.** High nominal yields with high inflation do not attract lasting capital.
- **Forgetting the counter-trend moves.** A 10% move against the cycle is normal inside a long wave, and it will stop out leveraged positions sized too large.

> **Risk warning:** Long-term views do not remove short-term volatility. Leveraged FX positions held for months incur swap costs and can suffer large drawdowns before a long-term view is proven right or wrong.
